# RGME Amiga Library Rights Audit — Batch 011

**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**Status:** Evidence-backed family audit; not legal advice.

## Executive Summary

Batch 011 gives RGME one of the strongest chain-of-title examples found so far.

In 2016 Starbreeze publicly announced acquisition of the **IP rights to the entire Cinemaware library** from CW Entertainment USA LLC d/b/a Cinemaware. The announcement explicitly listed the Cinemaware brand plus fourteen game properties and stated that the acquired rights included brands, websites, existing products and licenses.

In February 2024 Nordcurrent publicly announced that it had acquired the Cinemaware catalog from Starbreeze.

This produces:

```text
Cinemaware / CW Entertainment
             |
             | 2016 IP acquisition
             v
         Starbreeze
             |
             | 2024 catalogue acquisition
             v
         Nordcurrent
             |
             +--> Nordcurrent Labs
                     |
                     +--> Defender of the Crown:
                          The Legend Returns (2026)
```

Unlike many earlier batches, this is not merely an inference from company acquisitions. We have explicit transaction language and an explicit title list.

## 1. Why this batch is unusually strong

The 2016 transaction announcement explicitly names:

```text
Cinemaware brand
Defender of the Crown
Wings
The King of Chicago
Sinbad and the Throne of the Falcon
Lords of the Rising Sun
Rocket Ranger
It Came from the Desert
It Came from the Desert II: Antheads
S.D.I.
TV Sports: Basketball
TV Sports: Baseball
TV Sports: Boxing
TV Sports: Football
TV Sports: Hockey
```

It additionally says the acquired rights include brands, websites, existing products and licenses.

For RGME this is far stronger evidence than:

```text
Company B acquired Company A
therefore B probably owns Game X
```

because the relevant assets are actually enumerated.

## 2. Nordcurrent acquisition

Nordcurrent announced in February 2024 that it acquired the Cinemaware catalogue from Starbreeze.

Its public statement is unusually useful for RGME because Nordcurrent also described its intent: it wanted to bring the historical games to modern PC and console audiences and named *Defender of the Crown* as its first planned remaster.

This gives us not merely an owner candidate but a current company with an explicit **retro-modernization strategy**.

## 3. Defender of the Crown — validation and conflict

*Defender of the Crown* is almost the perfect conceptual validation of RGME:

```text
1980s classic
     ↓
modern rights acquisition
     ↓
new owner recognizes nostalgia value
     ↓
modernized new release
```

But that same fact makes it a poor first unsolicited RGME pilot.

In August 2026 *Defender of the Crown: The Legend Returns* launched for modern platforms through Nordcurrent Labs.

Therefore:

```yaml
rights_clarity: very_high
modernization_value: very_high
brand_recognition: very_high
current_commercial_activity: very_high
pilot_priority: low_for_first_outreach
```

The IP is already actively being modernized.

## 5. Wings

*Wings* is particularly attractive technically.

RGME could demonstrate:

- authentic Amiga execution;
- modern controller mapping;
- improved flight controls;
- save-state/resume;
- CRT and clean-pixel display modes;
- optional modern HUD;
- historical campaign presentation;
- accessibility features.

A future licensed enhancement could go much further without needing to replace the original preservation path.

## 6. Rocket Ranger

*Rocket Ranger* is an unusually good RGME showcase because its cinematic presentation maps directly onto the project's goal:

> preserve the original experience while modernizing the delivery layer.

Potential improvements include:

```text
gamepad mapping
modern save UX
display profiles
optional subtitle/accessibility layer
touch adaptation
modern launcher
historical metadata
```

without initially touching the original game logic.

## 7. It Came from the Desert

This title has strong showcase value because of its mixture of:

- adventure;

- action sequences;
- cinematic presentation;
- recognizable B-movie aesthetic.

It would test RGME across several interaction patterns within one title.

