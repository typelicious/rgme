# RGME Amiga Library Rights Audit — Batch 006
## Psygnosis → Sony Interactive Entertainment

**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**Status:** Evidence-backed family audit; title-specific chain of title remains decisive.

## Executive summary

Psygnosis is one of the most important families in the Amiga collection, but also one of the clearest warnings against naive corporate-successor inference.

Sony acquired Psygnosis in the 1990s, and some major Psygnosis properties have strong present-day Sony evidence. **Shadow of the Beast** is the clearest example: Sony Interactive Entertainment currently publishes the 2016 reimagining and the PlayStation legal information identifies the mark with Sony.

**Lemmings** also remains on a strong Sony rights route. The original was developed by DMA Design and published by Psygnosis. Current trademark records identify Sony Interactive Entertainment Europe Limited as owner of the LEMMINGS mark, while Exient publicly described Lemmings as Sony IP and currently publishes the mobile incarnation.

This batch therefore corrects an early research hypothesis: **Exient is not modeled as the owner of Lemmings. It is modeled as a current licensee/publisher unless stronger ownership evidence appears.**

That correction itself produces a useful RGME requirement: Rights Intelligence needs an auditable **claim lifecycle**, not merely a current value.

```text
hypothesis
   |
   v
unverified
   |
   +--> verified
   |
   +--> contradicted
           |
           v
        retracted
           |
           v
      superseded-by
       new claim
```

## Major findings

### Shadow of the Beast

This is one of the strongest Sony-lineage records in the whole Psygnosis family.

The modern PlayStation title explicitly returns to the original Amiga property, is published by Sony Interactive Entertainment and carries Sony-associated trademark language.

RGME policy:

- local BYOG: suitable;
- public original binary: blocked without permission;
- public remaster: blocked without license;
- modernization research: useful;
- licensing lead: Sony Interactive Entertainment.

The trilogy should nevertheless preserve release-level identity. Evidence about the modern franchise does not automatically establish the exact component ownership of every Amiga disk, soundtrack or piece of artwork.

### Lemmings

The rights graph is more informative:

```text
DMA Design
   |
   | developed
   v
Lemmings (1991)
   |
   | published / rights history
   v
Psygnosis
   |
   | Sony corporate succession
   v
Sony Interactive Entertainment
   |
   | licenses current mobile exploitation
   v
Exient
```

Current trademark evidence still points to Sony Interactive Entertainment Europe Limited.

Therefore:

```yaml
ip_owner_assessment:
  entity: Sony Interactive Entertainment Europe Limited
  confidence: high

current_licensee:
  entity: Exient
  scope: mobile/current publishing
  confidence: high
```

Do not collapse owner and licensee.

### The wider Psygnosis catalogue

Titles such as *Agony*, *Awesome*, *Blood Money*, *The Killing Game Show*, *Ork*, *Obitus* and others have strong historical Psygnosis relationships, but this batch deliberately does **not** promote them all to `Sony owns`.

For each title RGME should investigate:

1. original developer;
2. employment vs external-development relationship;
3. original publishing agreement;
4. copyright notices;
5. later assignments;
6. trademark ownership;
7. re-releases;
8. current licensing activity;
9. individual music/art rights where relevant.

## New Rights Intelligence requirement — Claim Lifecycle

Batch 006 reveals a feature missing from the earlier Rights Record model.

A rights claim should have:

```ts
type ClaimStatus =
  | "hypothesis"
  | "unverified"
  | "supported"
  | "verified"
  | "contradicted"
  | "retracted"
  | "superseded";

interface RightsClaim {
  id: string;
  subject: string;
  predicate: string;
  object: string;

  status: ClaimStatus;
  confidence: number;

  evidenceIds: string[];
  counterEvidenceIds: string[];

  supersedes?: string[];
  supersededBy?: string;

  createdAt: string;
  reviewedAt?: string;
}
```

This prevents an agent or researcher from silently rewriting history when better evidence arrives.

For an AI-assisted Rights Intelligence system this is essential.

## Product opportunity

### Poor early licensing targets

- Shadow of the Beast
- Lemmings

Both have major, active rights routes.

### Better rights-archaeology targets

The less actively exploited Psygnosis-era catalogue may contain significantly more interesting opportunities.

Titles with:

- identifiable original creators;
- no obvious current exploitation;
- fragmented but traceable rights;
- cult recognition;
- strong Amiga identity

should receive dedicated title-level audits.

These may ultimately be more valuable to RGME than famous franchises because a modernization license may actually be obtainable.

## Recommended RGME change

Add to the forthcoming Rights Intelligence specification:

```text
Rights Entity Graph
Rights Transfer Graph
License Graph
Claim Lifecycle
Counter-Evidence
Supersession
Confidence History
Human Review
```

The rights database then becomes a research system rather than a static spreadsheet.

## Batch conclusion

Batch 006 demonstrates three distinct patterns:

1. **Strong active Sony IP** — Shadow of the Beast.
2. **Sony-owned/licensed active IP with third-party operator** — Lemmings / Exient.
3. **Historical Psygnosis catalogue with unresolved title-level succession** — many other Amiga games.

That distinction is exactly the kind of structure RGME Rights Intelligence should preserve.
