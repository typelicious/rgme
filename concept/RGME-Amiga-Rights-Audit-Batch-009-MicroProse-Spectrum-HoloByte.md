## Executive Summary

Batch 009 establishes a critical RGME rule:

> **The current company using a historic publisher's name is not automatically the successor owner of that publisher's historical catalogue.**

The historic MicroProse/Spectrum HoloByte catalogue has split across several modern rights ecosystems. *Civilization* is now an actively exploited 2K/Firaxis/Take-Two franchise, while the revived current MicroProse has strong present-day continuity around selected heritage such as *B-17 Flying Fortress*. Other titles require their own chain-of-title research.

```text
Historic MicroProse
       |
       +---- Civilization --------> 2K / Firaxis / Take-Two
       |
       +---- selected legacy IP --> title-specific research
       |
       +---- Hasbro/Infogrames ---> portions of legacy catalogue
       |
       +---- B-17 lineage --------> current MicroProse activity
       |
       +---- brand/name ----------> revived MicroProse company
```

## 1. Civilization — the clearest split

The local collection contains *Civilization*. In 2026 the franchise is actively developed and published by Firaxis/2K within Take-Two. Historical MicroProse publication therefore cannot be used as evidence that today's MicroProse owns the franchise.

This requires RGME to distinguish `historical_publisher`, `current_franchise_route`, `current_ip_owner`, and `brand_operator`.

## 2. B-17 Flying Fortress — current MicroProse continuity

Current MicroProse materials explicitly connect new B-17 products with the classic MicroProse B-17 games. That gives RGME a strong current stewardship/licensing lead, though exact ownership of every component in the Amiga release must still be established.

This is strategically interesting: the revived MicroProse actively embraces simulation heritage and has a public publishing route, making it a more plausible eventual RGME partner than many large legacy-IP conglomerates.

## 3. Spectrum HoloByte and Tetris

Spectrum HoloByte's historic role in computer Tetris releases is not evidence of present ownership of the Tetris franchise. RGME must model old software copyright, publisher rights, franchise rights, trademarks, trade dress and current licenses separately.

## 4. Military, aircraft and motorsport layers

Titles involving F-19/F-117, Gunship, M1, Falcon, B-17 or Formula One introduce external layers. RGME must neither assume that every real-world designation is proprietary nor that all logos, manufacturer marks, photography and branded presentation are free to reuse.

The rights decision therefore remains use-specific and component-specific.

## 5. Temporal Rights Graph

Batch 009 makes temporal semantics mandatory.

```yaml
relation:
  type: OWNS_IP
  subject: organization-x
  object: game-y
  valid_from: YYYY-MM-DD
  valid_to: YYYY-MM-DD
  scope:
    components: [software, franchise]
  territory: [...]
  platforms: [...]
  evidence: [...]
```

Transfers should be explicit events, not inferred from today's corporate names.

An AI research agent must not be allowed to infer:

```text
A published X
B bought A
C later bought assets from B
therefore C owns X
```

unless evidence shows the relevant right actually passed through the required transactions.

## 6. Brand Continuity

RGME Rights Intelligence should add:

```ts
interface BrandContinuity {
  brand: EntityId;
  historicalEntity?: EntityId;
  currentOperator: EntityId;
  relationship:
    | "same-entity"
    | "corporate-successor"
    | "brand-acquisition"
    | "brand-license"
    | "revival"
    | "unknown";
  doesNotImplyCatalogueOwnership: true;
  evidenceIds: EvidenceId[];
}
```

This directly handles revived retro brands such as MicroProse.

## 8. Business-development angle

A future current-MicroProse approach should follow a narrow pilot strategy:

```text
1 rights-cleared public RGME demonstrator
2 BYOG MicroProse-era profiles
3 simulation-control modernization demonstrator
4 precise rights/request scope
5 approach current MicroProse
6 propose one title, not an entire catalogue
```

A credible pilot could ask only for browser execution, modern controls, authentic/CRT modes, saves and historical metadata, with deeper remaster rights negotiated separately.

## Batch Conclusion

Batch 009 establishes five reusable patterns:

1. historic publisher brand ≠ current catalogue owner;
2. one historic catalogue can split across modern rights ecosystems;
3. revived brands may still be valuable licensing routes for selected properties;
4. external real-world brands require separate rights layers;
5. rights relationships require temporal scope.
