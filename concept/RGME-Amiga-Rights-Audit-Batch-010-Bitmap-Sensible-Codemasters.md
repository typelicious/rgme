# RGME Amiga Library Rights Audit — Batch 010
## Bitmap Brothers / Sensible Software / Codemasters

**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**Status:** Evidence-backed family audit; title/release/component rights remain decisive.

## Executive Summary

Batch 010 contains two of the strongest current rights routes found so far.

### Bitmap Brothers → Rebellion

In 2019 Rebellion acquired the Bitmap Brothers brand and **entire portfolio**. Contemporary transaction reporting from the law firm involved in the sale explicitly describes the full portfolio transfer. Rebellion has since actively exploited the catalogue and released a new *Speedball* in 2026.

That makes Rebellion a high-confidence current licensing route for core Bitmap properties.

### Sensible Software → Codemasters → EA

Sensible Software was sold to Codemasters in 1999. Jon Hare has repeatedly described *Sensible Soccer* as a Codemasters-owned property. Electronic Arts then completed its acquisition of Codemasters in 2021.

This produces a strong present-day route for major Sensible properties, although exact copyright, trademark, historic data and third-party-license scope still has to be checked per release.

```text
Bitmap Brothers ──────> Rebellion
                           |
                           +--> active Speedball exploitation

Sensible Software ─────> Codemasters ─────> Electronic Arts
                           |
                           +--> Sensible catalogue route
```

## 1. Bitmap Brothers — unusually clean acquisition evidence

The 2019 transaction is valuable to RGME because the available evidence goes beyond a vague corporate acquisition.

The transaction was described as sale of the Bitmap Brothers **brand and entire portfolio** to Rebellion.

Core titles therefore receive a strong route:

```text
Xenon
Xenon 2: Megablast
Speedball
Speedball 2: Brutal Deluxe
Gods
The Chaos Engine
Cadaver
Magic Pockets
```

This still does not mean every historical third-party asset or music license automatically transferred. Component-level review remains necessary.

### Speedball is active IP

Rebellion released a new *Speedball* in January 2026.

Consequently:

```yaml
rights_clarity: high
current_commercial_activity: high
public_unlicensed_rgme_use: no
licensing_route: clear
licensing_attractiveness: potentially interesting
```

This distinction is important: a clear owner can be easier to approach even when the IP is actively exploited.

## 2. Rebellion as a potential RGME partner

Rebellion is strategically interesting because it publicly positions itself as custodian of historic game properties and has already brought classic Bitmap material to modern platforms.

That creates a potential long-term RGME pitch:

```text
Preservation-grade original execution
+
modern controls
+
browser delivery
+
historical metadata
+
optional enhancement packages
+
rights-controlled distribution
```

A pitch should not start with *Speedball*, however. A less actively exploited Bitmap title may offer a lower-friction pilot.

## 4. Sensible Soccer and football data

A public modernization of *Sensible Soccer* or *SWOS* creates more layers than game code alone.

Potential layers include:

```text
game software
Sensible / Sensible Soccer marks
historic club names
club badges
league marks
player names
player likeness/data
kit designs
sponsor marks
music
```

A private BYOG execution layer does not require RGME to republish all of those assets independently.

A public remaster could.

This makes SWOS an excellent test case for **rights-aware asset substitution**:

```text
Original BYOG mode
       |
       +--> original user-owned data

Public demonstration mode
       |
       +--> cleared generic teams
       +--> cleared names
       +--> replacement badges
       +--> replacement data
```

That pattern could later generalize to other licensed sports games.

## 5. Cannon Fodder

*Cannon Fodder* and *Cannon Fodder 2* are attractive RGME modernization candidates because they combine:

- mouse-driven controls;

The rights route is relatively clear through Sensible/Codemasters, but the titles are valuable enough that public use should remain licensing-first.

## 6. Codemasters proper

The library also contains classic Codemasters-associated material such as Dizzy-family and simulator titles.

EA's official Codemasters history still explicitly references early properties including **Dizzy** and **Micro Machines**, while confirming Codemasters became part of EA in 2021.

This gives us a strong current corporate contact route but not necessarily ownership of every third-party brand embedded in those games.

## 7. New concept — Licensing Feasibility Score

The accumulated batches reveal that **legal confidence** and **commercial licensing feasibility** are different dimensions.

For example:

```text
Speedball

rights clarity:       very high
current activity:     very high
owner identifiable:   very high
technical value:      high
license likelihood:   unknown
```

versus a dormant title:

```text
Dormant Game X

rights clarity:       medium
current activity:     low
owner identifiable:   high
technical value:      high
license likelihood:   potentially higher
```

RGME Rights Intelligence should therefore add:

```ts
interface LicensingFeasibility {
  rightsClarity: number;
  licensingRouteClarity: number;
  currentCommercialActivity: number;
  externalLicenseComplexity: number;
  rightsholderSize: number;
  catalogueFit: number;
  technicalDemoValue: number;
  brandRisk: number;

  score: number;
  confidence: number;
}
```

The score must never be presented as a legal conclusion.

## 8. Candidate-selection engine

This enables a future query such as:

```text
Find Amiga games where:

rights clarity > 0.75
AND current commercial activity < 0.35
AND licensing route clarity > 0.70
AND external license complexity < 0.30
AND RGME modernization value > 0.75
```

The result is no longer merely a rights database.

It becomes an **IP opportunity discovery engine**.

## 9. Potential Bitmap pilot strategy

A sensible future sequence would be:

```text
BYOG profile
    ↓
working RGME modernization
    ↓
video/demo evidence
    ↓
rights package
    ↓
Rebellion contact
    ↓
single-title pilot proposal
```

The proposal could initially request:

- official browser execution;
- modern controller support;
- save states;
- Authentic + CRT + Modern display modes;
- historical metadata;
- no asset replacement;
- no gameplay alteration.

Enhancement/remaster rights could then be negotiated separately.

## 10. Architectural addition

After Batch 010 the Rights Intelligence subsystem should formally distinguish:

```text
LEGAL CONFIDENCE
      ≠
LICENSING FEASIBILITY
      ≠
TECHNICAL MODERNIZATION VALUE
      ≠
COMMERCIAL OPPORTUNITY
```

Together these become a candidate-ranking model:

```text
Rights Evidence
      ↓
Legal Confidence
      ↓
Licensing Feasibility
      ↓
Modernization Potential
      ↓
Commercial / Preservation Value
      ↓
RGME Candidate Score
```

## Batch Conclusion

Batch 010 establishes three particularly valuable findings:

1. **Bitmap Brothers → Rebellion** is one of the cleanest portfolio-acquisition routes found so far.
2. **Sensible → Codemasters → EA** provides another strong modern licensing route.
3. Clear rights do not necessarily mean easy licensing; therefore RGME needs a separate **Licensing Feasibility Score**.

This moves Rights Intelligence beyond compliance and toward active project and licensing discovery.
