**Status:** Evidence-backed research batch; not legal advice

## Method

This batch enriches the RGME Rights Library using the local filename listing plus current public evidence. A filename proves that a named archive/variant occurs in the listing; it does **not** establish an exact release until content hashes are available.

`allow_local_byog` is an RGME operational policy for locally supplied content, not a blanket legal declaration. Public binary distribution, browser hosting, derivative distribution and commercial use remain blocked unless sufficient permission evidence exists.

## 1. Battle Isle

### Library hits

- `Battle Isle (Data Disks).lha`
- `Battle Isle.lha`

### Findings

- Original Amiga title: Blue Byte, 1991.
- Ubisoft's historic financial reporting documents its 2001 acquisition of Blue Byte and explicitly identifies *Battle Isle* as a Blue Byte series.
- Ubisoft currently markets *Battle Isle Platinum*.
- Working classification: copyrighted, actively exploited property; not public domain/“abandonware”.
- Near-term RGME route: local/BYOG execution. Public distribution or remaster requires permission.

## 2. Oil Imperium

### Library hits

- `Oil Imperium.lha`
- `Oil Imperium (DE).lha`
- `Black Gold (Reline).lha`

### Findings

- Historical evidence identifies reLINE Software and the 1989 release; Rainbow Arts also appears in publishing history.
- A secondary source states that rights are now held by “Archangel Studios”.
- The current `oilempire.com` / `oilimperium.de` imprint identifies **archangels studio / Oliver Maslo** and asserts copyright in the current Oil Imperium service.
- This makes that operator a credible **licensing lead**, but does not establish ownership of every right in the 1989 Amiga binary, source/code, graphics, music, manual or marks.
- For RGME this is a particularly interesting rights-outreach case because the current operator appears reachable and the IP is much less institutionally distant than Disney/Ubisoft.

### Action policy

| Action | RGME policy |
|---|---|
| Private/local BYOG execution | ALLOW by local policy |
| Binary redistribution | DENY pending chain-of-title |
| Public browser hosting | DENY pending chain-of-title |
| Reverse engineering | REVIEW |
| Patch distribution | REVIEW |
| Modification | REVIEW |
| Derivative/remaster distribution | DENY pending explicit license |
| Commercial use | DENY pending explicit license |

### Recommended rights outreach

1. Establish the legal identity behind the current Oil Imperium service.
2. Ask whether its rights cover the original 1989 Amiga/C64/DOS/ST editions.
3. Separate binary/code, graphics, music/audio, manual/box art and trademark/title rights.
4. Request permission for a private RGME modernization proof-of-concept.
5. Negotiate public browser demo, patch/remaster and commercial scopes separately.

## 3. Zak McKracken and the Alien Mindbenders

### Findings

- The listing contains base, German, French and Italian variants.
- Steam currently lists **Lucasfilm Games** as developer and **Lucasfilm, Disney** as publishers.
- GOG likewise commercially distributes the title under Lucasfilm / Disney.
- Current commercial versions use ScummVM. This demonstrates modern runtime viability, but not permission to redistribute the Amiga archive.
- RGME use: excellent local/BYOG technical showcase; unsuitable as a bundled public demo without permission.

## Batch conclusion

The three titles demonstrate three core research patterns:

1. **Battle Isle:** corporate successor and current exploitation are clear.
2. **Oil Imperium:** plausible small/current licensing lead, but incomplete chain of title.
3. **Zak McKracken:** major entertainment IP with clear active commercial exploitation.

For near-term RGME rights outreach, **Oil Imperium is the most interesting of these three**, because there is a plausible reachable operator to whom a narrowly scoped modernization proposal can be made. Battle Isle and Zak are strong BYOG/runtime targets.
